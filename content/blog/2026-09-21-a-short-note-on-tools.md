Every few months a new tool arrives promising to replace three of the ones I
already run. Sometimes it does. More often it replaces the parts I understood
well and leaves the parts I did not.

The test I keep coming back to is simple: when this breaks at an inconvenient
hour, will I be able to reason about it? A tool I can hold in my head beats a
faster one I cannot.

That is not an argument against new tools. It is an argument for paying the
learning cost deliberately, while nothing is on fire, rather than discovering
it in the middle of an incident.
