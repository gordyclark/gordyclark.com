## Philip Roth

The undisputed champion. Roth wrote like a man who had never once been told "maybe not," and the books are better for it right up until the moment they aren't. Every protagonist is the same horny, aggrieved professor-shaped fellow, and the women exist mostly to be wanted, resented, and then written about at length. Say what you like about the prose; it commits.

## Kim Stanley Robinson

A surprise on the list, because KSR is otherwise a hero. But a man who can make a hundred pages of terraforming geology gripping seems to lose all that nerve the instant two characters get close. The sex scenes read like a very sincere field report, filed by someone who has heard about the subject but has not seen it for himself.

## Haruki Murakami

Everybody falls into bed with the narrator, usually within a few pages of meeting him, and usually after he has made some spaghetti. The women are mysterious, uniformly gorgeous, and prone to sudden ear-related revelations. Their breasts get far more description than their motives. Beautiful books, awkward bedrooms.

## E.L. Doctorow

The weakest entry on the list is also the most forgivable, since Doctorow was busy writing real history into fiction and the sex scenes feel like paperwork he had to get through to reach the next chapter. Still counts.
