I'm Gordy Clark. I'm an engineer who writes, which mostly means I spend my working hours building software and my off hours trying to say clearly what I learned while building it. The two activities turn out to be the same activity wearing different clothes: both are about finding the smallest honest version of an idea and refusing to pad it.

Professionally I work with the team at WorkMind. I won't pretend the job is the interesting part of this site — the interesting part is the residue it leaves behind, the small opinions about tools and craft that only form after you've maintained something long enough to regret it.

This site is where those opinions go. It is a collection of essays, most of them about building things: why some designs age well and others rot, why the boring choice is usually the durable one, and why I keep reaching for plain files over clever systems. I write slowly and revise more than I'd like to admit, so expect a modest pace rather than a firehose.

There is no newsletter, no comment section, and nothing here trying to sell you anything. If a piece is useful to you, that's the whole transaction. If it's wrong, I'd genuinely like to know — the fastest way to improve an argument is to have someone poke a hole in it.
